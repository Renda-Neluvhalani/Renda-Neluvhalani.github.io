Can a simple model beat the bookmaker?

I built two models on five Premier League seasons, from 2021/22 to 2025/26, and tested them against Bet365's odds on the 2025/26 season.

The models were trained on the first four seasons (1,900 matches in all) and never saw the 342 test matches. The first was a logistic regression on each team's last five matches of form. The second was a Poisson model of goals scored, which rates each team's attack and defence. Before comparing, I removed Bet365's margin, 5.5% on average, so its odds became fair probabilities. I also measured everything against a baseline that always predicts the average home, draw and away rates.

37%of the gap between the baseline and the bookmaker closed by my Poisson model on log loss. The bookmaker was still the best forecaster.

What I found

The bookmaker was best

Lowest log loss and the closest calibration. My Poisson model beat the baseline and got 37% of the way there.

Form alone didn't help

The logistic regression on five-match form did slightly worse than the baseline. Long-run strength matters more.

−3.1% from 299 bets

The value-betting backtest lost 9.2 units. Better than the ~5.5% loss from random betting, but it couldn't beat the margin.

A bias towards away teams

222 of the 299 bets were away wins. The model underrated home teams, so it kept finding false "value" in their opponents.

Problems I solved

  • Data leakage. I shifted each team's results by one match before taking the five-match rolling average, so a form feature never includes the match it's predicting.
  • Date parsing. The source files write dates as day/month/year, so I parsed them with dayfirst=True.
  • Teams with no history. Promoted teams had no training seasons for the model to rate, so I excluded those matches.

What I'd try next

  • Retrain team strengths weekly instead of fixing them from the training seasons.
  • Raise the edge threshold from 5% to around 10%, since the model bet on 87% of matches.
  • Correct the home bias with the Dixon-Coles adjustment or a team-specific home advantage.

Planet Masase space station

An interactive 3D space station orbiting an icy planet, built with Three.js and Vite. The polaroid at the top of this page is the real project running live.

Ships fly on inclined orbits, the station rotates and tracks its own orbit, and you can switch lighting and shading models while it runs to compare them. Everything is built from primitive shapes in a scene graph, so each part can move on its own.

Scene graphA central hub and spine, two clusters of six docking modules, four rotating solar wings, end-mounted communication dishes, and five rockets on separate inclined orbits.
LightingA directional sun with a day and eclipse toggle, pulsing pink and blue docking beacons, a warm hub glow, a docking spotlight and a rotating searchlight.
ShadingFlat on the cargo modules, Gouraud on the structure and Phong on the metal core, rings, dishes and ships, all switchable while it runs.
MappingImage textures on the planet, walls, platforms and panels, a generated cube environment map for reflections, and a generated normal map for raised detail on the solar panels.
